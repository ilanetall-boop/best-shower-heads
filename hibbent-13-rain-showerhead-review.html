What We Liked

What stands out most about the Hibbent 13'' rain showerhead combo is the two-in-one layout at a price under $95. A 13-inch rain head and a handheld wand bought separately would typically cost more than this bundled price, and plenty of reviewers point out that having both options on one valve solves the common complaint of rain heads being awkward for washing your face or rinsing a child. The chrome-plated finish also gives it a look close to the pricier HammerHead model for less money, which matters if the rest of your bathroom hardware is already chrome. Running both heads off the same shared valve keeps the setup simpler than a full shower system with separate controls for each spray.

What Could Be Better

The biggest drawback on the Hibbent 13'' Rain Showerhead Combo is material. ABS with chrome plating will not hold up to scratches, corrosion, or hard water mineral buildup as well as the solid metal body on the HammerHead Solid Metal Handheld, and owners describe the chrome finish picking up water spots faster than they expected. The Combo also skips the cUPC certification that its sibling, the Hibbent cUPC Certified High Pressure, carries, so buyers in areas with strict plumbing codes should check local requirements before installing it. Neither issue rules out the purchase for most households, but they are the two factors that separate this model from the certified and metal alternatives in this lineup. A soft cloth and a non-abrasive cleaner will keep the chrome layer looking newer for longer, which matters more here than on an all-metal head since the plating is thinner than a solid finish.

Who Is It For?

You are the right buyer for this Hibbent 13'' rain showerhead if you want a rain head and a handheld wand without paying for two separate fixtures or two separate installs. If your home has hard water, or you plan to keep the fixture for a decade, the Delta 5-Setting HydroRain or the metal HammerHead alternative are worth the extra few dollars for better long-term durability. Skip this pick if local code requires certification on new fixtures, since the cUPC-certified sibling covers that requirement for only a few dollars more.
