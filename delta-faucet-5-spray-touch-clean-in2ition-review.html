Delta built the 5-Spray Touch-Clean In2ition around one idea: most bathrooms need both a fixed overhead shower and a handheld wand, and installing two separate fixtures to get both is overkill. This model mounts a fixed shower head and a detachable hand shower on the same valve, so you switch between the two without touching a wrench or calling a plumber. The body is chrome-plated ABS rather than solid metal. That keeps the price near $141.42 and the overall weight low, at some cost to the dense, all-metal feel you get from pricier fixtures like the Delta Modern 14 Series Square covered below. Its five spray settings run from a wide, full-coverage rinse to a tighter massage stream, giving you more range than a single-function head without adding a second product to your cart or a second hole in your tile.

The standout feature on the spec sheet is the touch-clean nozzle design. The spray holes sit on soft, raised silicone nubs that you wipe with a fingertip to knock loose mineral deposits. No brush, no vinegar soak, no descaling kit. At 1.75 GPM, the flow rate sits comfortably under the federal 2.5 GPM maximum. That keeps the model compliant, but it also means it is not a high-pressure upgrade for homes that already struggle with weak water pressure. If your current plumbing already delivers strong flow, the In2ition passes that pressure through cleanly. If low water pressure is already a known problem in your home, a dedicated high-pressure head, like the Hibbent pick covered in the alternatives section, will likely serve you better than this 2-in-1 format.

What We Liked

The Delta Faucet 5-Spray Touch-Clean In2ition stands out most for how much it simplifies a bathroom that needs two shower formats. You get a fixed head and a handheld wand built into one valve and one set of hardware, which also means fewer connections that can eventually leak. Owners who review the broader In2ition line consistently point to the touch-clean nozzles as the detail that saves the most hassle day to day, since clearing hard-water buildup takes a quick wipe instead of a maintenance session with a brush or descaling solution. The five-setting spray pattern also gives the handheld wand real range: it shifts from a wide rinse setting to a tighter massage stream for cleaning a tub or tile grout without you needing to switch tools. For the price, you are getting the flexibility of two shower head types without paying for, or installing, two separate fixtures.

What Could Be Better

The trade-offs with the Delta Faucet 5-Spray Touch-Clean In2ition follow directly from its price and format. The chrome-plated ABS body feels noticeably lighter than an all-metal fixture, and owners researching the line point to that lighter weight as their main durability concern, even though Delta backs the model with its standard warranty. The 1.75 GPM flow rate is the bigger limitation for most bathrooms: it complies with federal rules, but it will not compensate for weak existing water pressure the way a dedicated high-pressure head can. Because the unit splits its plumbing between a fixed head and a handheld wand, you are also relying on a single diverter valve to manage both functions, so a failure there affects your whole setup rather than a single fixture. None of these are dealbreakers on their own, but they are worth weighing against the convenience the 2-in-1 format offers.

Who Is It For?

The Delta Faucet 5-Spray Touch-Clean In2ition fits households that want one fixture doing two jobs, a shower to stand under and a handheld wand for rinsing kids, pets or a tub, without paying for and installing two separate shower heads. It also suits anyone dealing with hard water who wants to maintain the spray nozzles without scrubbing or soaking parts on a regular schedule. You are a weaker fit for this model if your main complaint is low water pressure, since its 1.75 GPM rating will not outperform your existing supply, or if you specifically want the denser, all-metal feel of a single-purpose fixture like the Kohler Forte three-function showerhead reviewed below.
